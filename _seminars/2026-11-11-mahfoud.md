abstract: |
  As the world pushes toward localized circular economies, Lebanon remains far from the full integration of complex Post-Consumer Recycled (PCR) and Post-Industrial Recycled (PIR) resins into meaningful plastic applications. This talk dissects the critical role of Dynamic Mechanical Analysis (DMA), focusing heavily on molten-state rheology, as a diagnostic and predictive tool for polymer blending. By decoding viscoelastic properties, we can precisely screen, modify, and optimize recycled plastic composites to match or exceed virgin material benchmarks. To showcase these capabilities, we compare two compatibilization routes that differ in structure and thus rheological output, validated via imaging, mechanical, and thermal testing. Finally, we examine three local recycling case studies that benefit the community, reinstating the importance of rheological testing while addressing some standout predictive limitations.
bio: |
  Dr. Elie Mahfoud is a Polymer Technical & Development Specialist at PACT, the dedicated R&D center of INDEVCO Plastics. He earned his PhD in thermosetting composite materials and manufacturing from the American University of Beirut's Mechanical Engineering Department, preceded by a Master's degree in Aeronautical Engineering from the University of Balamand. He subsequently transitioned to the plastic converting and compounding industry, pivoting his expertise toward polymer extrusion, blending, and recycling. His current work intersects directly with sustainability, leveraging advanced characterization techniques like melt rheology and DMA to pioneer high-performance recycled plastic composites tailored for a circular economy.
