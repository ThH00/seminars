abstract: |
  In this talk, we discuss learning low-dimensional models of nonlinear dynamical systems from data. We begin by recalling linear systems theory, reviewing how engineering concepts such as modes, mode shapes, and modal analysis amount to model reduction to invariant structures in the phase space of linear systems. We then review the theory of spectral submanifolds (SSMs). Under fairly general conditions, SSMs are the invariant manifolds that emerge in the phase space once nonlinearities are added to the system. As such, they allow model reduction to be rigorously extended from linear to nonlinear systems. This extension also covers forced nonlinear systems, since SSMs persist under time-dependent forcing. Finally, we move from theory to practice and show how SSMs can be learned from data using our open-source tool SSMLearn, with an emphasis on recent research. Examples include models learned from experimental data and from high-dimensional finite-element simulations.
bio: |
  Ahmed Amr Morsy is a postdoctoral researcher in the Nonlinear Dynamics group at ETH Zurich's Institute for Mechanical Systems. His research develops low-dimensional models that explain and predict nonlinear phenomena, such as bifurcations and chaos, in high-dimensional simulations and experiments. His work combines invariant manifold theory, data-driven modeling, and uncertainty quantification, with applications across engineering and the physical sciences. He received his Ph.D. in Mechanical and Process Engineering from ETH Zurich in 2025 and his M.Sc. in Civil Engineering from Politecnico di Milano in 2020.
