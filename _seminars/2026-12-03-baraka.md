abstract: |
  This talk will first introduce and motivate the need for machines that teach and learn from humans through rich and situated interactions, drawing on the speaker's human-robot interaction research over the past 10 years, spanning several labs and cross-disciplinary collaborations. In the second half of the talk, this work will be positioned in the broader landscape of alignment (see [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_alignment) for a layperson definition), arguing that human-AI alignment should be approached from the perspective of knowledge transfer, including implicit and embodied forms of knowledge in addition to symbolic ones.
bio: |
  Kim Baraka (AUB ECE alum '14) is currently a tenured assistant professor in the Social AI group at VU Amsterdam. He was a postdoctoral fellow in the Socially Intelligent Machines Lab at UT Austin, and holds a dual Ph.D. in Robotics from Carnegie Mellon University and the Instituto Superior Técnico in Lisbon, Portugal. His interdisciplinary research, at the cross-roads of human-centered robotics, machine learning, and dance, focuses on enabling robots and humans to teach and learn from each other through situated social interactions. He is part of the Hybrid Intelligence Center (which fundamentally researches hybrid human-AI systems, with applications to healthcare and education), an Associate Editor of the ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction, and a scientific board member of the Lorentz Center in Leiden. As a professionally trained contemporary dancer, he is particularly interested in new frontiers in robotics that draw inspiration from the performing arts.
