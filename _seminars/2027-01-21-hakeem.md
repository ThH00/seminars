abstract: |
  Gasoline turbocharged direct injection (GTDI) engines enable downsizing of spark-ignition (SI) engines, which results in significant fuel economy savings, especially in city driving conditions. However, injecting the fuel directly into the cylinder creates some challenges in edge cases, such as colder climates and shorter driving cycles. In such conditions, unburnt fuel impinges on the cylinder walls and accumulates in the engine oil, and later evaporates into the intake through the positive crankcase ventilation (PCV) system as unmetered fuel, which can disturb air-fuel ratio estimation.

  This seminar presents research at Ford Motor Company that characterized this problem and its implications for engine controls and design. Gas chromatography with mass spectrometry (GC-MS) was used to speciate the fuel hydrocarbons in oil samples taken during the accumulation and warm-up phases of a cold-climate vehicle test. Comparing these samples showed how different hydrocarbon components accumulate and evaporate.

  This work demonstrates how measurement noise on one system can create an opportunity for useful information on another: a patented model shows how hydrocarbons interfering with intake air sensors can reveal the level of fuel oil dilution. The seminar also shows how the automotive industry is the perfect example of interconnected and interdisciplinary systems, with interference between them being a feature, not a bug, because it enables endless opportunities for innovation and impact.

  Dr. Mohannad Hakeem is a researcher and inventor with 13 years of combined experience in the automotive industry, between Chrysler LLC and Ford Motor Company. His work on spark-ignition (SI) engine systems covered charging systems (alternators), exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) systems, and intake air measurement. He later worked on the virtual verification and validation (V&V) of SAE Level 2 advanced driver assistance system (ADAS) features. He is a named inventor on more than 70 patents, about ten of which have been implemented in production. Dr. Hakeem holds a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Wayne State University (2011) and B.E. (2002) and M.E. (2005) degrees in Mechanical Engineering from the American University of Beirut. Since returning to Lebanon, he has worked as an independent life coach and trainer, providing online career coaching and life design services.
